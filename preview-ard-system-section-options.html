The right system depends on where the water is coming from

Three systems, and the one you need depends on how water is getting in. Pick the one you were told you need, or let David work it out on site.

For water at the wall and floor joint

Interior French drain

A channel around the interior perimeter, perforated pipe bedded in clean stone, routed to a sump pit. Water that would build pressure against your walls is intercepted and redirected.

Typically 2 to 3 days for a standard basement.

For water entering through the wall

Exterior waterproofing

Excavating the foundation perimeter, applying a membrane to the exterior wall, and backfilling with drainage aggregate so water moves away from the structure.

More involved, and not always warranted. David will say so if it is not.

For a pump that cannot keep up

Sump pump and backup

Pumps sized to the actual inflow rate of your basement, with battery backup that keeps running when the power goes out, which is exactly when you need it.

The fix depends on how water is getting in. These are the three we see most in West Essex.

What you see
Water at the joint where the wall meets the floor
Usually means

Hydrostatic pressure under the slab

Saturated clay holds water against the foundation. The fix is an interior French drain that relieves the pressure, not sealant.

What you see
Damp patches or seepage through the wall itself
Usually means

Water entering through the wall

Exterior waterproofing: excavate the perimeter, membrane the wall, backfill with drainage aggregate so water moves away.

What you see
The pump runs constantly, or quits in a storm
Usually means

A pump that cannot keep up

Undersized, failed, or discharging where the water returns. Pumps sized to real inflow, with battery backup.
